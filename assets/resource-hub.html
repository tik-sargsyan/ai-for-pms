2. The one skill: the R·C·T·C prompt

R ole
Tell it who to be. "You are a senior product manager at a bank."
C ontext
Give it the facts, the files, the constraints. The more real context, the better the output.
T ask
One clear ask, and the exact output you want. "Draft a one-page PRD with edge cases, as a table."
C ritique
Close the loop. Ask it to check its own work, or you check it, then iterate once. Never ship the first draft.
Rule of thumb: a weak answer is almost always a weak prompt. Add role, add context, ask for a format, then critique.

5. The rules that keep you safe

  • Verify anything that matters. For any fact, number, or claim: check the source, or do not ship it.
  • Least privilege. Give an agent only the tools its job needs. Read-only whenever you can.
  • You keep the verdict. AI does the volume. You decide what is right and what ships.
  • Guard your data. Never paste customer data, KYC or AML files, internal models, or credentials into a public AI.
  • Build the judge. For anything AI generates at scale, write a rubric and grade it before you trust it.

6. What to use, and when

  • Daily work and building: Claude Code, on the Pro plan. This is the core.
  • Quick chat and drafts: the Claude app or claude.ai in the browser.
  • Shipping a product: Claude Code plus VS Code plus Vercel.
  • Want to explore more, for free: Google Antigravity (Gmail login, no cost). Codex only if you already pay for ChatGPT.
  • Models: default to the latest and most capable Claude for hard reasoning, and a faster model for routine drafts. Newer models can reason step by step, which helps on logic, math, and code.
