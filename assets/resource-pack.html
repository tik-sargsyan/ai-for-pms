01 The one skill: the R·C·T·C prompt

A weak prompt gets a weak answer. Four moves turn any AI into a sharp thinking partner.
R · Role

Tell it who to be. "Act as a senior PM."

C · Context

About you + the situation. The step everyone skips.

T · Task

Exactly what you want, and in what format.

C · Critique

Make it question you and push back, not just please you.

The pattern, assembled
Act as a senior product manager. I'm a [your role, your context]. [What you want, and the format]. Ask me clarifying questions first, then answer, then critique my thinking and tell me what to do next.

02 Principles that keep you ahead

  • Verify, don't trust. AI predicts plausible text. Check every fact, number, and quote before you use it.
  • Iterate. Never ship the first answer. The third reply is usually the good one.
  • The 5-50 rule. ~5 exchanges to calibrate it to you; ~50 variants when you want range and creativity.
  • Assistant vs operator. Chat advises; an operator (Claude Code, Codex) opens tools and does the work. Reach for the operator when you want it built, not described.
  • Guard your data. Never paste secrets, customer PII, or confidential company data into a public AI.

03 Prime your AI once

Paste this at the start of a project or a custom instruction, so every answer fits you.
Your product copilot
You are my product copilot. About me: I'm a [role] at [company], working on [product] for [users]. Constraints: [time / tech / brand]. How I like answers: structured, skeptical, with a source or a flag when you're unsure, and a clear recommendation (not a menu of options). Before answering, check if you have enough context; if not, ask first. Confirm you've got this.

04 Steal these prompts

Copy, swap the [brackets] for your real details, and iterate.
1 Synthesize research into themes
Act as a senior product researcher. Here is user feedback / interview notes / reviews: [PASTE] Cluster it into 4-6 themes. For each: a one-line summary, the underlying pain, how many sources mention it, and 2 real supporting quotes. Mark anything you're inferring vs directly stated. End with the 3 biggest opportunities.
2 Draft a PRD from rough notes
Act as a senior PM. Turn these rough notes into a PRD: [PASTE] Include: problem, target user, goals & non-goals, user stories with Given/When/Then acceptance criteria, edge cases and failure modes, open questions, and success metrics. Ask me up to 5 clarifying questions first, then write it.
3 Answer a data question, and verify it
Act as a skeptical data analyst. Dataset: [ATTACH/PASTE]. Question: [YOUR QUESTION]. Answer it, then show your work in plain English: which rows you included and excluded, the exact filters, and what you counted (people vs events, date range). Then argue why your answer might be wrong and what would change it.
4 Simulate a user interview
Act as [a specific persona: e.g., a busy ops manager at a mid-size logistics firm]. I'm researching [problem]. Answer as this person really would, with genuine objections and messy priorities. Stay in character and push back where they would. After 8 exchanges, break character and tell me the 3 insights I should have caught.
5 Exec update in your voice
Act as my chief of staff. Turn this into a 6-line exec update for [audience]: [PASTE status] Lead with the decision or ask, then progress, risks, next step. Keep my voice: direct, no hype, no filler. Give me two versions: one for our VP, one for the wider team.
6 Build the judge (eval a feature)
Act as an evaluation designer. I'm building an AI feature that [does X]. Write a scoring rubric to judge its output quality: 4-6 criteria, each on a 1-5 scale with what each score looks like. Then give me 5 test cases (including 2 hard edge cases) with the ideal answer for each, so I can measure it before shipping.

05 The modern AI stack, in one glance

Models — Claude / GPT / Gemini + open. Pick per task; the frontier moves monthly.
MCP — a standard plug so AI can use your tools and data.
Agents — AI that takes multi-step action with tools.
Skills — reusable abilities you teach an AI once.
APIs — the same models, built into products.
Operators — Claude Code, Codex: they build and ship, not just chat.
